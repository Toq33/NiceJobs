1. Product overview

NiceJobs connects hospitality professionals with verified hotels and hospitality businesses. It combines focused job discovery, structured employee profiles, employer pages, vacancy publishing, applications, and recruiter communication.

5. Employee account and profile

5.1 Account creation

Collect full name, one primary phone number, one primary email, and authentication details.

Verify phone and email before enabling key account functions.

Enforce one account per normalized phone and email using server-side/database uniqueness rules, not only frontend validation.

Provide secure recovery and verified contact-change processes; handle duplicate-account appeals without revealing whether a specific email/phone is registered.

Do not require a national ID number at basic registration. If identity checks become necessary, collect only what is justified, restrict access, encrypt it, set retention rules, and never show it publicly.

6. Business / hotel organization model

Separate the organization from the people who manage it. One hotel group may own multiple brands and properties; each organization may have multiple authorized members. A person can have one employee account and separately hold a role in one or more organizations.

6.1 Organization information

Legal business name and public-facing hotel/brand name; parent company or group where applicable.

Property type (hotel, resort, restaurant, cruise, catering, or other hospitality employer), address, city, country, map location, website, official business email, logo, cover image, and description.

Optional details: hotel category/star rating with source, facilities, property size, departments, staff benefits, accommodation/meals, training, and career development.

Verification evidence and review status are private administrative data. The public badge must describe the scope of verification and must not imply every employer claim is guaranteed.

6.2 Organization roles

Every action must be checked on the backend against the user's active organization membership and role. Remove access promptly when a member leaves the organization. There must be one role active at anytime to continue the daily and normal process.

7. Business / hotel profile

Overview: description, location, website, type, verified status, and parent group where applicable.

Open jobs: active vacancies, filters, deadlines, and direct application links.

Workplace and culture: team photos, facilities, values, benefits, training, and employee development.

Departments and properties: front office, housekeeping, F&B, culinary, sales, HR, finance, engineering, and other relevant departments.

Updates: official employer announcements and hiring campaigns from authorized business members.

Follow hotel: employees can follow the organization and opt into relevant job notifications.

Contact details: official contact channels and public location; never expose recruiters' private contact details by default.

8. Job posting and vacancy lifecycle

8.1 Job fields

Field group

Required / recommended data

Identity

Job title, department, hiring organization/property, vacancy count, job description.

Work conditions

City/location, on-site/hybrid/remote, employment type, shift/schedule where relevant.

Compensation

Salary min/max, currency, pay period, benefits/service charge where applicable; clearly label undisclosed salary.

Requirements

Experience, education, skills, certifications, languages, responsibilities.

Practical details

Deadline, expected start date, accommodation/meals, visa sponsorship where applicable, screening questions.

Publishing controls

Draft/published/paused/closed status, created by, approval/moderation state, timestamps.

8.2 Job lifecycle

Draft → Preview → Publish → Active → (Pause or Edit) → Closed/Expired. Only authorized organization members can create or edit jobs. The system records who changed a vacancy and when. Closed or expired vacancies stop accepting applications and are clearly labeled.

8.3 Job-card actions

Apply: if logged out, prompt sign-in and return the user to the job after authentication. If signed in, show the application form, selected CV, screening answers, consent/confirmation, and submit.

Share: share a public job URL or copy it; never include private applicant data in the shared link.

Save: store the job in the employee's saved list. Remove expired jobs from active search or mark them closed.

9. Applications and applicant tracking

Status

Meaning / next step

Submitted

Application saved and timestamped; confirmation sent to employee.

Under review

Recruiter is reviewing the CV/profile.

Shortlisted

Candidate selected for further consideration.

Interview invited / scheduled

Interview details and calendar information shared.

Offer

Employer records an offer or next-step status; avoid implying legal acceptance unless implemented.

Hired

Recruiter records a successful outcome.

Rejected

Employer closes the application and may send a respectful notification.

Withdrawn

Employee withdraws; record event and update recruiter view.

Job closed / expired

Vacancy no longer accepts new applications; existing applications remain available to authorized recruiters.

Every status change should be timestamped and attributed to an authorized actor. The employee sees the appropriate status and messages; private internal recruiter notes are never shown to the employee. Employers should have vacancy-specific candidate lists, filters, CV access controls, and an application event history.

10. Messaging and notifications

Employee inbox

Employer inbox

Conversations with authorized recruiters; application/job context; interview invitations; unread count; attachments; archive; block/report controls.

Conversations grouped by vacancy/candidate; candidate profile and application status; interview scheduling; templates; internal notes separated from candidate-visible messages; team assignment.

Messaging rules: users must be authenticated; employer senders must have active organization permissions; access is restricted to conversation participants and authorized support roles; implement spam limits, report/block controls, secure attachment handling, and audit events for sensitive actions.

Notifications can be in-app and email initially. Notify users about application submission/status changes, new recruiter messages, interview invitations, saved-search job alerts, and followed-hotel vacancies. Let users manage optional notification preferences.

11. Admin, safety, privacy and trust

Business verification: review organization evidence and representative affiliation; provide pending/approved/rejected states and an appeal route.

Job moderation: review reports, suspicious or duplicate listings, misleading compensation, impersonation, and recruitment-fee scams.

User safety: report/block, rate limits, account suspension/appeal, and clear moderation records.

Privacy: collect minimum necessary data, explain purpose, restrict CV/document access, support account/data deletion requests, define retention periods, and keep private information out of public profiles and URLs.

Security: secure password handling or established authentication, least privilege, server-side authorization, encryption in transit and at rest where supported, backups, monitoring, and audit logs.

Legal readiness: obtain appropriate Bangladesh legal/privacy advice before launch, especially for personal data, employment practices, cross-border expansion, and identity verification.

12. Advertising and monetization

The initial prototype can show ad placeholders without implementing billing. Potential revenue options after validating the marketplace include paid featured jobs, employer subscriptions, clearly labeled hospitality-industry ads, and recruitment events.

Desktop: two side-ad slots only if the main job feed remains readable; sticky behavior must not obstruct content.

Mobile: remove sidebars or place sponsored cards in the feed with clear labeling.

Ad targeting should use appropriate contextual or consented preferences; never sell CVs, NID details, phone numbers, or private messages.

Verification status cannot be purchased. Sponsored placement must not be presented as proof of employer legitimacy.

18. Prototype acceptance criteria

A job seeker can find a vacancy using keyword/location/department filters and understand salary and requirements.

A signed-in employee can complete a simulated application and see it in My Applications.

An employee can view/edit profile sections and understand who can see their profile/CV.

A recruiter can create a simulated job, review applicants, update a status, and see the workflow reflected in the dashboard.

A hotel can show multiple authorized team members and a clear verification state.

The user can open a conversation from a job/application context and understand who they are messaging.

The core experience works on desktop and mobile, with empty/error/success states.

The prototype clearly labels simulated actions and does not imply real accounts, messages, or applications were created.

19. Out of scope for the first prototype

Production authentication, real OTP/email delivery, and real CV storage.

Live messaging infrastructure and real notifications.

Real payment processing, ad billing, or subscription enforcement.

AI candidate scoring, advanced salary analytics, and large-scale recommendation systems.

Full LinkedIn-like social feed, public endorsements, and complex connection graphs.

International legal/tax/localization support beyond the initial Bangladesh-first design.

20. Immediate next steps

Use NiceJobs as the working brand and create a small design system: colors, type, spacing, buttons, inputs, cards, badges, and navigation.

Confirm the first job departments and realistic sample vacancies with local hospitality users.

Wireframe homepage, search results, job details, employee profile, employer profile, create-job form, applicant list, and inbox first.

Connect the employee and recruiter journeys before designing advanced features.

Test the prototype with job seekers and hotel recruiters; revise based on observed friction.

Only after prototype approval, finalize database rules and begin production development.
